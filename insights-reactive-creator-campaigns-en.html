Reactive creator campaigns for major events: how they work

Building blocks, risks and a practical example from the 2026 World Cup.

In short

In a reactive campaign, not every piece of content is fixed in advance. You agree on the framework, creators, formats and approval rules, and publish content as events unfold. This is how PANA's World Cup 2026 campaign produced 39 pieces of content with four creators and more than 4.3 million views, implemented within a few weeks from idea to rollout.

By PANA Agency · Updated: October 14, 2026

What is a reactive campaign?

Traditionally, campaigns are planned weeks in advance. At a tournament, however, the topics of conversation change daily. Reactive campaigns pick up these moments while they are still relevant.

When does it make sense?

  • For major events such as a football World Cup, when the audience is particularly attentive.
  • When a brand wants to show that it takes part in what is happening.
  • When several platforms and formats are to be covered.

The building blocks

  1. Small, well-briefed group of creators: Those who know the topic and the brand need less coordination.
  2. Framework briefing: Instead of scripts, there are guardrails, core statements and dos and don'ts.
  3. Fast approvals: Fixed contacts and clear deadlines.
  4. Format toolkit: Short videos, stories and other formats that can be produced quickly.
  5. Ongoing coordination: Regular feedback on what works.

The risks

  • Approval time: The longer approvals take, the less reactive the campaign is. Especially in regulated industries this has to be clarified early, see creator marketing in regulated industries.
  • Timing: A topic is only relevant for a short time.
  • Brand fit: Not every event suits the brand. Clear guardrails help with the selection.

Example from practice: World Cup 2026

For a leading neobroker, four football creators produced 39 pieces of content on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, mostly published in response to the course of the tournament. All BaFin-relevant approvals and labeling requirements were taken into account. Result: more than 4.3 million views. All details are in our case studies.

Checklist for your next reactive campaign

  • Define the event and period
  • Select and brief the creators
  • Clarify the approval process with fixed contacts
  • Define formats and platforms
  • Set success criteria and reporting up front
